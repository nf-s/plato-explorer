# Plato Explorer handoff

Plato Explorer is a public, static browser for exploring **where SEP articles mention people and ideas**. A reader can follow a connection from the graph to the sentence that produced it, read the article in context, and return to the graph. The app also searches the complete text independently of the graph.

## Decisions to preserve

- **Source and identity.** The structured Parquet supplies the article text and editorial related entries. The December 2023 JSONL supplies author credits. The fetch script verifies both files against pinned checksums. The build records the primary file’s SHA-256 and graph counts in the local `web/data/philosophy/build-report.json`. [Sources and provenance](data-model.md#source-selection)
- **What a connection means.** Each pair retains text mentions and explicit Related entries in each direction. Node size reflects incoming mentions. Line weight reflects mentions between the pair. The reader, graph and **Mentions** panel use the same stored matches. A mention of a person in a text is evidence of their name appearing there. Interpretations such as intellectual influence require reading the passage.
- **Specific ideas should remain discoverable.** Generic terms such as Time, Object and Truth dominated the first graph. The default hides mentions _to_ targets found in over half the articles. Minimum mentions starts at five, which also reduces drawing work. People, Topics and Keywords are independent checkboxes. Checking People and unchecking Topics while focused on Agency keeps Agency as the centre. A keyword-only overview can legitimately contain nodes with no edges.
- **People and words without pages matter.** Synthetic nodes such as Carl Jung and Music lead to the real articles that mention them. Use word and document frequencies to find candidates, then review their meanings and variants. An established topic, including one with a Wikipedia page, is a useful nomination. Ambiguous names and ordinary words require corpus checks. The [link audit](link-audit.md) explains how to measure both gains and false matches.
- **Visual and interaction preferences.** Use cool grey backgrounds, white panels, dark text, restrained slate accents and functional copy. The graph toolbar holds Network/List, the large shared search field and Filters. The user removed the logo and separate title bar to give the graph more room. Controls use a muted slate accent. Article links are blue and always underlined, with stronger hover and focus styling. Computed communities help arrange the overview. “Colour by” offers estimated subject, node type, connection count and “No categories”. A hover badge identifies Article, Person or Keyword.
- **Following connections.** After selecting a node, Shift-click a neighbour or use its **Mentions** button. The latter is the touch and keyboard route. The original proposal to click individual edges proved impractical in dense graphs. Choosing a passage opens the source with the sentence and matching words highlighted. **Original on SEP** opens the publisher's entry. The address records the open article, block and passage, so a passage can be linked from outside the app. The mobile graph, Details, reader and section-jump history have specific restoration checks in the [mobile guide](mobile-navigation.md).
- **Article previews.** Hover or focus an internal reader link for 550 ms, or press and hold it, to see its opening paragraph and full directional mention counts. Graph filters do not change these counts. Related-entry flags remain separate from text mentions. Arrow Down on a focused link moves focus into the card. Escape, Close, outside presses and reader scrolling dismiss it without adding history. A normal click still opens the network; **Read article** in the card opens the linked entry and Back restores the original reading position. Previews share the reader's validated article loader and bounded cache. People and keywords without an article show counts and **Connections** without requesting a nonexistent file.
- **One app, several realms.** The user asked for a single app where a reader picks a realm, with relationships shown only within the realm and links between realms for shared subjects. Every realm shares `web/` and the graph and search builders, and is stored locally as static files under `web/data/<id>/`. Philosophy is offered from two sources, the SEP and Wikipedia, as separate realms with the same name. The realm menu and the links between realms tell them apart by source. Names, source credits, notices, default filters and subject colours come from `realms/<id>/realm.json`. Keep realm-specific wording out of the shared files. Addresses without a realm open Philosophy, which preserves links shared earlier. [Realms](realms.md)
- **Access, cost and attribution.** The user chose a neutral public Pages address because the account's Workers hostname includes `nf-s` and changing the account namespace would affect other apps. Private Google OAuth with administrator grants was an earlier deployment and remains the general MA3-style pattern for new NFS apps. The SEP is not to be published, so a release leaves the SEP realm out of the public files and the published app has the Wikipedia realms only. [Restricted realms](realms.md#restricted-realms) Production serves a public realm's graph and search files as static assets and each article from an R2 bucket through one Pages Function, with no D1 reads for a visitor. The user did not want an article's download to include other articles, and asked for a storage change once a separate Pages project per realm proved to conflict with Cloudflare's terms. [Where a release keeps the data](realms.md#where-a-release-keeps-the-data) Retain the article credits, original links, copyright and independence notices. Crawler directives request exclusion and cannot guarantee that public material will never be scraped.

## Release record and verification

On 18 November 2026 the SEP realm was withdrawn from the published app, at the user's request, because the SEP's terms do not allow its entries to be republished on a public website. [Restricted realms](realms.md#restricted-realms)

- **Change.** A release now stages the Wikipedia realms only. The public realm list and the public realms' links no longer name the SEP realm. Every address the app writes now names its realm, because the default realm differs between a local build and the published app.
- **Checks.** 122 JavaScript and 53 Python tests pass. The staged release was run in Wrangler's local Pages runtime.
- **Live checks.** Requests for the SEP realm's files were refused on the canonical address and on the new deployment's own address. The public realm list holds the seven Wikipedia realms. In the browser the app opened Wikipedia's Philosophy, made no `/api/` requests and logged no errors.
- **Earlier deployments deleted.** The twelve deployments made before this release served the SEP graph and articles at their own addresses. They were deleted the same day and their addresses return 404.
- **README.** The README and copyright notice describe the published app as the Wikipedia realms and say why the SEP realm is not published. The NFS home launcher's entry already names the canonical address and was not changed.

On 16 November 2026 an Art realm was added at the user's request. WikiProject Visual arts rates quality and not importance, so a scope can now nominate articles by quality class; they are kept on the same link test as Low-rated articles. The realm has 9,898 articles, 5,293 of them people, 268,914 connections and a 12.5 MiB graph. Eleven aliases that the link measurements accepted wrongly, such as “fired” for Pottery, are blocked in `realms/art/mention_rules.json`, and eight false links to the SEP realm are excluded. Wikidata's query service refused requests during an outage, so the realm has no Wikidata seeds. It was browser-checked locally at desktop and 375px widths: graph, reader, full-text search and links between realms, with no console errors. Its 9,898 articles were then uploaded to the bucket and the app was released to `plato-network` at the user's request. On the live site the realm index lists eight realms, 62 sampled Art articles were present and ten matched the local files, and the graph, reader, full-text search and a link to another realm worked with no console errors. A phone-width layout was not checked live.

Later again on 15 November 2026 the storage for releases changed and a second Philosophy realm was added from Wikipedia, at the user's request.

- **Storage.** The six per-realm data projects described in the next entry were created and uploaded, then withdrawn before the app used them. Cloudflare's Self-Serve Subscription Agreement does not allow use intended to circumvent a service's limits, and the projects existed only to stay under the 20,000-file limit. Articles now go to an R2 bucket, one object each, and a Pages Function serves them. Everything else is static in the app's project. [Where a release keeps the data](realms.md#where-a-release-keeps-the-data)
- **Wikipedia philosophy.** The realm `philosophy-wikipedia` has 9,723 articles, 4,244 of them people. Its links to the SEP realm were reviewed where the titles differ, and 22 false matches were excluded.
- **Matching fix.** The graph builder also matches a title without its leading “The”, so The Man claimed every “man” in Society and The Philosopher every “philosopher”. The shorter phrase now has to pass the same link test as the title, and it stays with an article that has it as its whole title. The six Wikipedia realms were rebuilt.
- **Checks.** 106 JavaScript and 49 Python tests pass. The Function was run in Wrangler's local Pages runtime with a local bucket before release. The new realm was browser-checked at desktop and 375px widths.
- **Released.** The user enabled R2 and the bucket `plato-network-articles` was created. All 55,589 articles were uploaded in about four and a half hours. Cloudflare answered ten single uploads with a 500, and Wrangler stops a batch at the first failure, so the script now sends 100 articles at a time and resends a failed batch. The app was then released to `plato-network`.
- **Live checks.** The realm index lists seven realms. An article came back identical to the local file and compressed, with the crawl headers and an ETag; a matching `If-None-Match` returned 304, a missing article 404, a POST 405 and a HEAD 200. A random 30 articles in each realm were present and five of each matched the local files. `/_routes.json` and `/_worker.js` return the app page, and the page still carries the Pages analytics beacon. In the browser, the reader, full-text search with excerpts, links between realms and a 375px layout worked with no console errors. A 320px layout and the installed iPhone app were not checked, and the Function's request counts were not read from the dashboard.
- **Conditional requests.** The first live check found a 500 when `If-None-Match` held text R2 cannot parse. The Function now sends the article in full in that case. The fix was released and checked live.
- **Data projects deleted.** The six `plato-network-<realm>` projects were deleted once the user confirmed. `plato-network` and the earlier `psyche-network` remain.
- **Psychology project retired.** At the user's request `psyche-network` now redirects every address to the Psychology realm of the combined app. An old address with a node opened that node there. The NFS home launcher's Psychology entry still names the old address and reaches the app through the redirect.

Later on 15 November 2026 four Wikipedia realms were added: Economics, Society (sociology and anthropology), Religion and Mythology. The user did not want an article's download to include other articles, so the article packs were withdrawn in favour of one file per article, and a release now gives each realm's data its own Pages project. [Where a release keeps the data](realms.md#where-a-release-keeps-the-data) The change passed 100 JavaScript and 41 Python tests and local browser checks of the six realms at desktop and 375px widths. It was not released.

On 15 November 2026 the two explorers were merged into one app with a realm menu. The change passed 98 JavaScript and 39 Python tests and browser checks of both realms at desktop, 375px and 320px widths. It was released to `plato-network` the same day and both realms were checked live. The separate psychology Pages project still serves its earlier build.

On 13 November 2026 the first psychology build held 7,994 articles and 278,274 connections in 8,147 uploaded files, with a 12.6 MB graph. It passed 97 JavaScript and 39 Python tests and browser checks of the graph, mentions, reader, search and a 375px layout. The refactored Plato build produced byte-identical graph and article files.

On 4 November 2026 the mobile **Contents** release passed 75 JavaScript and 27 Python tests. Live touch checks in WebKit covered 320px and 390px widths, section selection, exact scroll restoration, and rotation. These were browser-engine checks. Physical iPhone installation and interaction were not exercised by the automated runs.

For the next change, use `npm run check` and `npm test` with the generated data present. For changes to matching, save the previous graph first and run the corpus audit after rebuilding both data and search. For reader or navigation changes, exercise the actual taps and browser Back/Forward in a touch-enabled WebKit session. The targeted scenarios are in the guides above.

Source and documentation live in the [GitHub repository](https://github.com/nf-s/plato-explorer). Raw exports, generated files, audit output and credentials stay local. A push publishes source changes. Deployments require an explicit request and an agreed destination. Check redistribution rights before uploading material from the corpus. Pages receives an allowlisted build by direct upload. On 18 November 2026 the user asked for the README to carry a large link to the public app and screenshots from the Wikipedia realms only, which replaced the earlier rule against hosted addresses in the repository.
